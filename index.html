The Problem. The Process.
The Result.

Company A

Facility management · Lagos · 2026 · 38 buildings · 9 operations staff · 34 vendors · ~412 work orders a month

About 39 staff hours back every week, record errors down to effectively none — 0.3%, roughly one record a month — and the system paid back in seven months.

  1. Problem

    Nobody could say how long a job had been open.

    Work orders arrived by phone and email, went into a notebook, and were chased from memory.

    Work orders arrived by phone and email, went into a notebook, and were chased from memory. Eight landlord reports were built by hand — two staff, six days a month — landing between the 8th and the 15th, with no evidence attached to anything.

  2. Process

    We followed one work order end to end.

    Every hand-off mapped and timed across 412 monthly jobs. Three were the same information retyped.

    Every hand-off mapped and timed across 412 monthly jobs. Three of them were the same information being retyped — notebook, spreadsheet, report. Each retyping step carried its own error rate, and each one could be removed.

  3. Solution

    Raised once, by an incoming email.

    The incoming email raises the work order itself. It routes by trade and location, and a clock starts.

    The incoming email raises the work order itself — nobody reads it and retypes it. It routes to a vendor by trade and location and a clock starts, the job and its deadline going back out by email. Reminders escalate on their own — vendor, supervisor, Head of Operations. Closing requires a photograph. On the 1st, every report assembles itself, evidenced.

  4. Result

    Nobody opens a spreadsheet.

    Thirty-nine hours a week come back to the operation, and roughly one record a month carries an error.

    Thirty-nine hours a week come back to the operation, and roughly one record a month carries an error. Supervisors work from phones; the Head of Operations works from one dashboard. Reports leave on the 1st, evidenced.
