Core ingestion & data syndication pipeline

A West African bulk freight and maritime supply chain consortium was paying seven analysts to retype port paperwork. We replaced the retyping with a system that reads the documents itself, checks them against three sources that had never spoken to each other, and raises the invoice the same day.

1.0 · The system
  • The business. A trans-West African bulk freight logistics and maritime supply chain consortium.
  • Where it operates. Administrative and financial headquarters on Victoria Island, Lagos. Marine operations and the high-volume intake desks sit at the port complex in Apapa.
  • Scale. 64 active heavy-duty haulage vehicles, and lead logistics partner to 14 Tier-1 FMCG multinationals.
  • Paper volume. Around 1,200 multi-page documents every month — customs Single Goods Declarations, terminal delivery orders, shipping line release notes, port gate passes and driver waybills — each one indexed, validated and reconciled.
2.0 · Where it was leaking
  • Seven analysts on the wrong work. A specialist tier of seven full-time operations analysts was pulled off fleet management entirely, spending a combined 4.5 hours of every working day on manual verification and transcription.
  • The intake bottleneck. Operators read unstructured text off physical waybills, crumpled thermal slips and low-resolution phone photographs sent by drivers over WhatsApp at port check-in, then keyed it line by line into isolated spreadsheets.
  • An 8.2% error rate. Typos, inverted tracking prefixes and missing container seal numbers. Every one of them broke the automation downstream.
  • What it cost. Tracking blind spots, incorrect driver mileage payouts, late demurrage monitoring, and a four-to-five day lag before billing could be raised. Millions in working capital sat locked up every week in completed trips that were unbilled and unverified.
3.0 · What we built

A zero-human-input workspace for document extraction, parameter isolation and cross-system reconciliation.

  • It reads what it is given. A context-aware processor built into the master operations desktop. Dispatchers drop in anything — low-contrast scans, skewed phone photographs, native digital manifests — and it reads the text without manual pre-processing.
  • It checks three systems that never spoke. Container IDs and seal numbers against the master customs manifest. Driver log sheets against automated fuel station receipts, to isolate and flag financial over-allocation. Delivery timestamps against signed client SLAs.
  • It only asks when something is wrong. One dashboard instead of spreadsheets built from scratch. Verified matches commit themselves; anomalies and outliers surface in high-contrast telemetry for fast sign-off.
4.0 · What it returned — 60-day audit
  • Reclaimed capacity. Manual data entry is gone. Work that took the operations floor the whole morning now runs, validates and commits in under 15 minutes a day — 42 staff hours back every week.
  • Errors removed structurally. Transcription discrepancies fell from 8.2% to a stable 0.2%, taking billing friction and FMCG client disputes with them.
  • Cash in sooner. The gap between delivery and invoice went from five business days to same day — working capital freed, and the revenue pipeline closed.
