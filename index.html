Core ingestion & data syndication pipeline

A West African bulk freight and maritime supply chain consortium was paying seven analysts to retype port paperwork. We replaced the retyping with a system that reads the documents itself, checks them against three sources that had never spoken to each other, and raises the invoice the same day.

42staff hours back every week
0.2%data errors, down from 8.2%
Same dayinvoicing, down from 5 days
1.0 · The system
  • The business. A trans-West African bulk freight logistics and maritime supply chain consortium.
  • Where it operates. Administrative and financial headquarters on Victoria Island, Lagos. Marine operations and the high-volume intake desks sit at the port complex in Apapa.
  • Scale. 64 active heavy-duty haulage vehicles, and lead logistics partner to 14 Tier-1 FMCG multinationals.
  • Paper volume. Around 1,200 multi-page documents every month — customs Single Goods Declarations, terminal delivery orders, shipping line release notes, port gate passes and driver waybills — each one indexed, validated and reconciled.
2.0 · Where it was leaking
  • Seven analysts on the wrong work. A specialist tier of seven full-time operations analysts was pulled off fleet management entirely, spending a combined 4.5 hours of every working day on manual verification and transcription.
  • The intake bottleneck. Operators read unstructured text off physical waybills, crumpled thermal slips and low-resolution phone photographs sent by drivers over WhatsApp at port check-in, then keyed it line by line into isolated spreadsheets.
  • An 8.2% error rate. Typos, inverted tracking prefixes and missing container seal numbers. Every one of them broke the automation downstream.
  • What it cost. Tracking blind spots, incorrect driver mileage payouts, late demurrage monitoring, and a four-to-five day lag before billing could be raised. Millions in working capital sat locked up every week in completed trips that were unbilled and unverified.
3.0 · What we built

A zero-human-input workspace for document extraction, parameter isolation and cross-system reconciliation.

  • It reads what it is given. A context-aware processor built into the master operations desktop. Dispatchers drop in anything — low-contrast scans, skewed phone photographs, native digital manifests — and it reads the text without manual pre-processing.
  • It checks three systems that never spoke. Container IDs and seal numbers against the master customs manifest. Driver log sheets against automated fuel station receipts, to isolate and flag financial over-allocation. Delivery timestamps against signed client SLAs.
  • It only asks when something is wrong. One dashboard instead of spreadsheets built from scratch. Verified matches commit themselves; anomalies and outliers surface in high-contrast telemetry for fast sign-off.
4.0 · What it returned — 60-day audit
  • Reclaimed capacity. Manual data entry is gone. Work that took the operations floor the whole morning now runs, validates and commits in under 15 minutes a day — 42 staff hours back every week.
  • Errors removed structurally. Transcription discrepancies fell from 8.2% to a stable 0.2%, taking billing friction and FMCG client disputes with them.
  • Cash in sooner. The gap between delivery and invoice went from five business days to same day — working capital freed, and the revenue pipeline closed.

Email-driven workflow engine & operations hub

A commercial architecture studio was running 28 developments out of an email inbox, with three senior people spending eighteen hours a week reading it and retyping what they found into project trackers. We made the inbox itself the engine: mail arriving now opens the project, builds the timeline, assigns the work and reports on it, with nobody in the middle.

18hours back every week
0%design rework, down from 12%
2 minto open a project, from 4.5 hours
1.0 · The system
  • The business. A high-end commercial architecture and urban design studio.
  • Where it operates. Headquarters in Lekki Phase 1, Lagos, running projects across Ikoyi, Eko Atlantic and international real-estate corridors.
  • Scale. 28 commercial and premium residential developments at once, an in-house team of 18 senior architects, structural engineers and interior designers, and more than 50 specialist sub-contractors.
  • Mail volume. Over 800 client revisions, regulatory approval notices, structural briefs and new enquiries arriving in executive mailboxes every week.
2.0 · Where it was leaking
  • Eighteen hours a week on administration. The Head of Architecture and two project coordinators spent that time reading mail, updating trackers, making folders, assigning tasks and writing status emails to stakeholders.
  • Work sat in inboxes. Onboarding requests, design briefs and critical structural feedback waited days before anyone moved them onto a project board.
  • 12% rework, every design phase. Architects kept working on superseded drawings because a client's revision email had not been flagged in time. That meant missed regulatory deadlines and constant executive micro-management just to find out where anything stood.
3.0 · What we built

A zero-touch command engine driven entirely by the mail the studio already sends and receives.

  • Projects open themselves. The engine watches the main business inbox. A signed proposal arriving extracts the client's details, creates the client record and spins up a dedicated project workspace.
  • Timelines build themselves. It reads the scope out of the onboarding thread, lays out the design programme against the standard milestones — schematic design, design development, construction documents — sets the dates, and sends the client their onboarding brief.
  • Work assigns itself. From the project type and its keywords, the hub finds who is free, allocates the design tasks and emails each architect and engineer their own responsibilities and links.
  • Leadership is told, not asked. When staff reply to a milestone thread or submit files, the engine reads the update and moves the timeline. The Head of Architecture gets a weekly digest, and anything within 48 hours of slipping raises an immediate warning to the team and to leadership.
4.0 · What it returned — 45-day audit
  • 94% less administration. Onboarding a client, building the timeline and assigning the work went from 4.5 hours per project to under two minutes of background processing.
  • Version-control errors gone. Because workspaces, timelines and files stay current straight from the mail, rework caused by missed client revisions fell from 12% to zero.
  • Eighteen hours a week back. Time the Head of Architecture had spent chasing staff for updates, returned — while clients report markedly higher satisfaction from milestone notifications that now arrive without fail.
